  • Body-size-based allometric trophic network (ATN) (Brose et al. 2006) models predict interactions from consumer–resource size relationships.
  • However, body size is only one determinant of trophic interactions; other traits and ecological constraints may be important.
  • Aim: quantify how often body-size-based ATN models predict interactions absent from empirical metawebs, and test whether network structure can nevertheless be recovered.
  • Prediction: ATN models will generate a high proportion of forbidden interactions, even when their broad structural properties resemble those of empirical metawebs.

Template metawebs

  • Great Britain: European metaweb (Maiorano et al. 2020); subsampled to mammal species checklist from the UK Mammal Society - this is because some of the links in the european metaweb are ‘forbidden’ due to co-occurrence limitations but could be feasible if species are treated as co-occurring. So this would have inflated the number of perceived forbidden links in the ATN. For Canada it is less of a problem since it is not constrained by co-occurrence.
  • Canada: probabilistic metaweb from Strydom et al. (2022).
  • Body sizes: mammalian body-mass estimates from PanTHERIA (Jones et al. 2009).
  • Briefly describe species matching, taxonomic harmonisation and missing-data handling.

ATN models and forbidden interactions

  • Generate probabilistic and binary ATN networks using body size, varying \((R_{\mathrm{opt}}), (\gamma)\) and, for binary networks, the interaction threshold.
  • For probabilistic metawebs, account for uncertainty by sampling interactions.
  • Define forbidden interactions as ATN links absent from the corresponding metaweb.
  • Quantify the percentage of ATN links that are forbidden across parameter combinations and simulation replicates.

Network structure comparison

  • Compare a small set of structural metrics between ATN networks and metawebs (e.g. connectance, degree distributions and nestedness, where appropriate).
  • Assess whether similar structural properties can coexist with poor recovery of specific species-level interactions.

Body-size models predict many forbidden interactions

  • Report the percentage of predicted ATN links absent from the metaweb.
  • Show sensitivity to \((R_{\mathrm{opt}}), (\gamma)\), and the binary interaction threshold.
  • Compare results between Great Britain and Canada.

Similar structure does not imply correct interactions

  • Compare structural metrics between ATN networks and metawebs.
  • Highlight any cases where structural similarity coexists with low pairwise interaction agreement.

Discussion

  • Body-size-based ATN models can predict interactions that are unsupported by the reference metawebs.
  • Structural similarity, if observed, does not necessarily imply recovery of the correct species-level links.
  • Discuss the limitations of body size as a sole predictor and the potential importance of additional traits and ecological constraints.
  • Note that metaweb absence is not definitive proof that an interaction is biologically impossible; results measure disagreement with the reference interaction data.
